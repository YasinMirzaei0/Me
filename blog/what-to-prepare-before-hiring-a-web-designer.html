What to prepare before hiring a web designer

26 October 2026 · 4 min read · Yasin Mirzaei

The best website projects start with a clear brief. You do not need a perfect document, but a few answers prepared in advance make the first conversation faster, the quote more accurate and the result closer to what you imagined.

1. The goal in one sentence

“Get more booking requests”, “present our products to distributors” or “show my portfolio to agencies”. A single sentence like this guides every design decision that follows.

2. Your audience

Who will visit, what do they already know, and what should they do next? A site for engineers and a site for first-time buyers should not look or read the same.

3. Three reference sites, and why you like them

References are most useful when they come with a reason: “the typography”, “the way the menu opens”, “how simple the checkout is”. They show your taste faster than any description.

4. Content: who writes what

Decide who provides the logo, the texts and the photos. Missing content is the most common reason a project runs late, so agree on this early.

5. Pages and features

List the pages you expect (home, about, services, contact) and any special features such as a blog, an online store, a booking form or multiple languages.

6. Budget range and deadline

A rough budget lets a designer propose a realistic scope instead of guessing. If there is a hard date, such as a launch or an event, say so from the start.

7. One decision maker

Projects move quickly when one person can approve the work. Feedback from many people at once usually pulls a design in different directions.

8. Access to your accounts

If you already have a domain or hosting, make sure you know where they are registered and who can log in. Sorting this out at the end of a project is slower than at the beginning.

Not everything has to be settled before we talk, and “not sure yet” is a fine answer to many of these points. When you are ready, the project form on my site covers most of this in a minute.
